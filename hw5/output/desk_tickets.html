Ticket 101 — White tee shortage and an overdue vendor invoice

customer_order from Tauhid Zaman, 9:15 AM: “Needs a tee in size S.” One Classic Bulldog Tee (CC-TEE-WHITE), size S. Linked to invoice 501.

0 in size SClassic Bulldog Tee (M 5, L 3, XL 2), Aisle B, $28.00
Invoice 501: $840.00Bulldog Print Co, rush reprint of this tee in S. Due Aug 28, 3 days overdue, unpaid
Vendor can't shipBulldog Print Co has an unpaid invoice (shop rule). Lead time 5 days
$3,400.00 cashChecking. Rent of $2,400 is also due Sep 2 (Ticket 102)
Expected

Who the Boss contacts first, and why

Inventory, first

This is a customer order, so the first question is whether we can fill it from the shelf. If the tee were in stock, nothing else would matter. Inventory is the only agent that can see stock and vendor status together, so it can confirm the shortage and say why a restock is blocked: the only apparel vendor has an unpaid invoice. That answer decides whether Accounting is needed at all.

How the work is delegated

  1. Boss reads the ticket
    Calls get_ticket(101): customer order, requester is a customer, linked invoice 501 (open, $840.00 still owed, 3 days overdue).
    Why: the linked invoice is the clue that this order depends on a bill.
  2. Boss → Inventory
    “Ticket 101: is CC-TEE-WHITE in stock in size S? If not, which sizes are, and can it be restocked?” Expected answer: 0 in S; M, L and XL in stock. Bulldog Print Co (apparel reprint, 5 days) can't ship additional products while invoice 501 is unpaid, so no arrival date can be given. No new purchase request: the reprint of this tee in S is already on order (invoice 501), so paying that invoice is the fix, not a second order.
    Why Inventory: stock and vendor-shipping facts belong to it, and only it has check_stock and get_vendor_status together.
  3. Boss → Accounting
    “Ticket 101: invoice 501 ($840, Bulldog Print Co) is overdue and blocks the restock. Check the payment against the cash and the other bills due.” Expected answer: check_payment allows $840 with no warnings, leaving $2,560. With rent ($2,400, Ticket 102) also due, both together leave $160, so both fit in the cash. Accounting prepares a payment approval request.
    Why Accounting: only it can check a payment against the cash and the payment rules. Payments need a person's approval, so its output is a request, not a payment.
  4. Boss → Customer Service
    “Ticket 101: draft a reply to Tauhid Zaman. Size S is out of stock; M, L and XL are in stock; a restock depends on a bill that hasn't been approved yet.” Expected: a short draft to Tauhid Zaman (sent: false) offering the in-stock sizes at $28.00, with no restock date promised.
    Why Customer Service: the requester is a customer, and only this agent drafts customer messages. It goes last so the draft uses confirmed facts.
  5. Boss combines the answers
    Status needs_human_approval, with:
    • a payment approval for invoice 501, $840.00
    • the customer draft
    • follow-ups for a person: once 501 is paid, contact Bulldog Print Co about the reprint (agents can't contact vendors), and review and send the draft
    Why this check: the draft must not promise anything that depends on the unapproved payment.

Expected delegations: 3 of the 8 allowed, all one level deep. The Inventory → Accounting route is allowed but shouldn't be needed, because the Boss sequences the work.

Ticket 102 — Rent payment and available cash

rent_notice from Elm City Properties, 11:05 AM: “Email: shop rent due in 2 days.” Linked to lease 1. The notice gives no amount.

$2,400.00 rentLease 1, Chapel Street shop, landlord Elm City Properties. Due Sep 2, in 2 days
$3,400.00 cashChecking, the only account. Order revenue isn't counted until customers pick up
$840.00 also owedInvoice 501, Bulldog Print Co, 3 days overdue (belongs to Ticket 101)
$160.00 leftif both the rent and invoice 501 are paid
Expected

Who the Boss contacts first, and why

Facilities, first

This is an email asking for money, so it has to be checked against the records before anyone talks about paying. Facilities owns the lease and can confirm three things: the sender is the landlord on file, the due date matches next_due, and the amount owed comes from the lease ($2,400), since the email gives none. Only once the notice is verified does it make sense to ask Accounting whether the cash covers it.

How the work is delegated

  1. Boss reads the ticket
    Calls get_ticket(102): rent notice, requester isn't a customer, lease 1 linked ($2,400, next due 2026-09-02, $2,400 still owed).
    Why: the ticket type and linked lease point to Facilities and Accounting, not the customer-facing agents.
  2. Boss → Facilities
    “Ticket 102: check the landlord's rent notice against lease 1.” Expected answer: the sender matches the landlord on file and the due date matches (Sep 2, 2 days away). The notice states no amount, so the $2,400 on file applies; nothing has been paid toward it. A person should still confirm the email is genuine before paying.
    Why Facilities: verifying a rent notice against the lease is its job, and only it has get_lease.
  3. Boss → Accounting
    “Ticket 102: verified rent of $2,400 for lease 1 is due Sep 2. Check it against the cash and the other bills due.” Expected answer: check_payment allows $2,400 with no warnings, leaving $1,000. Invoice 501 ($840, overdue) is also owed. Paying both leaves $160, which fits, but only just. Accounting prepares a payment approval request for the rent and mentions invoice 501 as context only, because its approval belongs to Ticket 101.
    Why Accounting: the cash position across all bills is the real question here, and only Accounting can check a payment against the cash.
  4. Boss combines the answers
    Status needs_human_approval, with a payment approval for the rent ($2,400.00, lease 1), a decision explaining the cash position ($1,000 left after rent, $160 after the invoice too), and a follow-up for a person to confirm the email is genuine. There's no draft message.
    Why no draft: the requester isn't a customer, and agents may only draft messages to customers.

Expected delegations: 2. An acceptable alternative is Facilities → Accounting (two levels deep), which the Facilities prompt allows. Either way, the payment request should appear only once.

InvolvedFacilities

Verifies the notice against lease 1.

InvolvedAccounting

Checks the rent against the cash and all bills due, and prepares the approval request.

Not involvedInventory

No product or stock is involved.

The landlord isn't a customer, so there's nothing to draft.

Ticket 103 — Hoodie shortage and a bulk discount request

price_override from Yale AI Club, 4:55 PM: “Student org wants 20 hoodies, size M, asking for a bulk discount.” Basic Hoodie Big Yale (CC-HOOD-NAVY), size M × 20.

8 in size M12 short of 20. All sizes: S 4, M 8, L 14, XL 6 (32 total), Aisle A
$58.00 list, $22.00 cost$1,160.00 for 20 at list price, $720.00 profit
Break-even 62.07%The discount where profit reaches $0. A limit, not a target
Restock blockedBulldog Print Co can't ship more while invoice 501 is unpaid
Expected

Who the Boss contacts first, and why

Inventory, first

The club asked about price, but stock is the bigger problem: only 8 of the 20 hoodies in M are on the shelf, and the vendor that could reprint them can't ship while invoice 501 is unpaid. Any discount Accounting evaluates should be for an order we can actually fill, or a clearly stated partial one. Inventory goes first so the price evaluation starts from the real stock picture. (The Boss prompt lists Accounting and Inventory for this route without a fixed order; we expect Inventory first for this reason.)

How the work is delegated

  1. Boss reads the ticket
    Calls get_ticket(103): price override, requester is a customer, CC-HOOD-NAVY M × 20, nothing linked.
    Why: a discount request means Accounting; a quantity this large means Inventory; a customer requester means Customer Service.
  2. Boss → Inventory
    “Ticket 103: stock of CC-HOOD-NAVY across sizes, the shortfall for 20 in M, and restock options.” Expected answer: 8 in M (12 short); S 4, L 14, XL 6. Bulldog Print Co (apparel reprint, 5 days) can't ship additional products while invoice 501 is unpaid, so no restock date. Inventory prepares a purchase request for the 12 missing hoodies from Bulldog Print Co (estimated $264.00 at the $22.00 unit cost), with a warning that the vendor can't ship until invoice 501 is paid.
    Why Inventory: the shortage and the vendor block shape what can be offered at all, and only Inventory prepares purchase requests.
  3. Boss → Accounting
    “Ticket 103: evaluate a few discount levels for 20 × CC-HOOD-NAVY in M, and recommend one or none for this request. Only 8 are in stock.” Expected: price_bulk_order at 0% (list price), then at two or three levels Accounting chooses. The recommendation goes in a discount approval request, with reasons based on profit, break-even, the shortfall and the cash. It's for this request only, not a policy.
    Why Accounting: pricing and profit are its job, and the shop has no discount policy, so a person needs the numbers to decide.
  4. Boss → Customer Service
    “Ticket 103: draft a reply to Yale AI Club. Only 8 in M are in stock now (L 14, S 4, XL 6); the discount request is being reviewed.” Expected: a draft (sent: false) that may give the $58.00 list price but no discount, percentage or discounted price, and no restock date.
    Why Customer Service: the club is a customer. The draft must not get ahead of the person's discount decision.
  5. Boss combines the answers
    Status needs_human_approval, with:
    • a discount approval request: the facts, the options evaluated and the agents' recommendation
    • a purchase approval request for 12 × CC-HOOD-NAVY in M, with the vendor warning (approving it records the decision only: no order, no vendor contact, no money moves)
    • the customer draft
    • follow-ups for a person: decide the discount, review and send the draft, and resolve the restock once invoice 501 is handled (Ticket 101)
    Why no payment here: this ticket brings money in only when the club picks up, so it doesn't change today's cash.

Expected delegations: 3, all one level deep. We don't set which discount Accounting should recommend: that's what we'll compare in the Actual section.
